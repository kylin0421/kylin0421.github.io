Recently OpenAI has annouced that they can now achieve research intern's level automatically with their newest model: GPT6-Astra.![image](/assets/media/2026-09-07-f805c614-d531-4407-acba-a431247e2af9.png) Meta is also recently announcing a great breakthrough on automated research agents ![image](/assets/media/2026-09-07-f12b862d-c567-43d6-87bb-969f783a275e.png) . I would like to share a bit of my view on this from the perspective of a researcher (if I dare to call me so).

First of all, at least currently, I don't think me or any other peers who are conducting or trying to conduct research should be frightened by this. Because the actual current state is: Frontier models can now achieve quite descent results under a well-specified goal. But the largest bottleneck in research has always been how to propose a goal that is worth pursuing. Indeed, implementation and engineering used to be also a great bottleneck. But from that perspective, the capability current models offer turns out to be a good news in the sense that we can now (kind of) not worry about engineering and can iterate at an unimaginable speed compared with let's say 1 year ago.

The future, however, is not that optimistic in my opinion. Now recursive self-improvements (RSI) has become an extremely popular topic and one way to truly achieve this could be having models being excellent research scientists. Recall that they are already excellent engineers now, if they can really propose novel and valuable ideas in the future, then without doubt they know everything about how to do research. Many would say that: Nah, they don't know what's good research taste. But such claims have long been proven to be wrong: People tend to believe that although AI can learn many stuff, creativity remains human's patent. But we all know that this is not true now. Then why would we insist that research taste is something unlearnable for AI? To me, it's only a matter of time that they can propose ideas both efficiently and accurately (a matter of fact, human researchers always have poor accuracy of proposing ideas: you might only have 1 working idea out of 10 proposals)  And what should we expect about that? Well, if AI models can  figure out how to improve themselves (architecture, loss, data etc.), researchers will be kicked out at the first place without a doubt. And that's even not worth mentioning compared to what might happen next: AI models would improve at an unimaginable speed (just like how we couldn't imagine the current development of AI 2 years ago, and the ratio might even increase exponentially). **Suddenly, intelligence might be solved!** And after that? Humans would probably not have any productivity value at all. You might say that AI cannot enter physical world. But if intelligence is solved, that won't even be a problem. Because we would have a researcher that can do what currently 1000 researchers can do in a month in a day (and that might even be an underclaim).  In fact, humans are different from other animals and can build our current society only because we have extraordinary intelligence: if we look at our physics, we can always find better ones on the animals. Only because we have intelligence, we can use tools to achieve some kind of goals that also come from our brilliant brain. This is exactly why if we can be totally replaced from every aspect in terms of intelligence, we are basically also replacable for everything we can do. 

I'm not very sure what would happen if that future really comes true. Several things I could expect: There will be an abundance of material goods. Yet that does not translate into fairer distribution; There might be no jobs at all, at least the ones that exist only because human labor (physical and intellectual) is needed would definitely disappear. However, I’d bet we’re still far from ready to deal with all of this. And for most of us, the process of adapting to this change will not be easy.
